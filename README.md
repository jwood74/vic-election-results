# Victoria Votes 2026

Independent, table-first tracker for Victoria's Legislative Assembly election on Saturday 28 November 2026. It covers all 88 districts, party first preferences, provisional 2CP leads, declarations, a browser-local watchlist and voting-centre drill-downs. The majority threshold is 45 seats.

District and booth tables use abbreviated, party-coloured headers and pinned name cells. Click a party heading to sort by its result. The district table can show first-preference percentages, votes or swings; the booth table can switch between counts and shares for first preferences and TCP. Swing stays in percentage points. Use **Closest TCP** in the district sort menu to see the narrowest current TCP margins first.

Open **My Predictions** (`?view=predictions`) to pick winners from the VEC's nominations across any of the 88 districts. Picks are saved in the current browser. Lock your pre-count set manually, or it closes when the count starts (6 pm Melbourne time on election day at the latest). The separate **Live picks** set stays editable for seats that have not been declared. Use the filters to find seats you still need to pick or picks that are behind the dashboard forecast. Only official VEC declarations count toward the score; TCP locks and forecasts are provisional. Pick all 88 seats to see if any party reaches the 45-seat majority in your forecast.

Try `/?demo=1&view=predictions` for six simulated district counts and separate demo storage. Rebuild the bundled nominations with `node scripts/build-candidates.mjs` if the VEC updates the candidate list.

The district table shows the current TCP leader's share and swing against the final historical preference distribution. District and projected TCP swings require a final two-candidate result and the same pair of sides. Declared districts are marked beside their names.

**Predicted result** estimates the final TCP share. It combines reported TCP with an estimate for first-preference ballots awaiting TCP, using observed preference flows from comparable completed counts (or historical aggregate flows when needed). For unreported votes it applies vote-weighted matched TCP swing to the historical mix of outstanding booths and vote types. Where only first preferences are available, the matched swing can use estimated TCP, and the projection may assume the historical pairing until a current pair is published. Remaining formal votes are estimated from historical turnout and the size of known unreported booths. Changed pairings, withheld counts and inconsistent tallies can prevent a forecast.

**Predicted party** shows Uncertain, Leaning or Likely based on count coverage, matched booths and how the lead holds up under different assumptions. Leaning requires at least two matched booths, 10% estimated formal coverage and a lead that survives a 10-point adverse shift on modelled votes. Likely requires four actual TCP matches, 35% formal coverage, 15% actual TCP coverage and a lead that survives a 20-point shift plus up to 5% more turnout. These are scenario thresholds, not calibrated probabilities. **Gain**, **Hold** or **TCP Locked** requires an official enrolment figure and a published TCP lead strictly larger than enrolment minus the TCP votes already counted. Gain and Hold compare with the previously declared winner; the lock is conditional on the published pair and tally. **Declared** comes from the VEC.

The statewide seat bar and **Seats by Party** table show the same outlook. Each seat appears at its highest confidence level, from Leaning through Likely, Locked and Declared, with darker colours for stronger calls. Uncertain and unreported seats remain unallocated. The table shows ALP, LIB, NAT, GRN and ONP, plus a single Other row when needed.

Each electorate's **Race summary** shows the current and projected TCP shares and the predicted party. Its count breakdown separates published TCP, first preferences awaiting TCP and estimated votes still to report. It reports the number of matched booths used in the forecast and, for a locked race, compares the published lead with the enrolment-based upper bound.

The district page’s collapsed **Your prediction** card shows the party you picked for that seat. Expand it to see the candidates and status of your saved pre-count and live picks.

The **Sportsbet favourite** heading below the race summary names the market option with the shortest odds, or lists joint favourites when tied. Expand it to see all quoted district-winner prices, shortest first. The supplied CSV has no capture timestamp. The snapshot is independent of the count forecast and **My Predictions**. To replace it with a newer CSV of the same format, run `python3 scripts/build-sportsbet-odds.py /path/to/odds.csv` and include the regenerated `public/data/sportsbet-odds-2026.json` in the deploy.

The district page’s **2022 winner** card shows the VEC’s declared winner and, where published, the final preference distribution or a labelled indicative TCP tally. Narracan shows its 2023 supplementary election result. Rebuild these results with `node scripts/build-historical-results.mjs`.

**Modelled win chance** estimates the probability that the projected leader finishes above 50% for the published TCP pair. Inspired by [Antony Green’s election-night analysis talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h_0bRylRZg0), it treats matched booths as clusters rather than counting each ballot as an independent observation. The model uses the spread of observed booth swings, their effective sample size and the share of votes still being modelled, with uncertainty allowances for preference flows, turnout and differences in which booths report. It uses a Student’s t approximation and requires at least four matched booths with published TCP, at least three effective booths after weighting, and 10% of estimated final votes in TCP. These estimates have not been calibrated on timestamped historical counts. A high percentage does not trigger a mathematical lock, which still uses the official upper bound.

Choose **Maximise booth table** for a full-width view with an electorate selector. Search, sort and the display mode carry over when opening or closing the view, and when switching electorates. A party-column sort is specific to its electorate and resets when switching electorates.

The maximised view pins the published district count above the booth rows and can filter to election-day booths, declaration votes or rows with reported results. Published postal, absent, provisional and other pooled vote categories can be searched, sorted and opened like booths. Prepoll appears by centre when available or as a pooled total; the TCP page may publish only the pooled prepoll figure. Pooled swings compare the same vote category, with TCP swing requiring the same two sides.

## Preview locally

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Open <http://127.0.0.1:8888>. Use `ARCHIVE_PREVIEW=1 npm run dev` to exercise the viewer with six **labelled 2022 archive districts** while there are no live results. That mode is local only and deliberately suppresses swing. Use `npm test` for parser and calculation tests, and `npm run check:source` for a public-site smoke test. That command checks for the 2026 index first and falls back to the 2022 archive for validation only.

To preview the outlined TCP swing columns with simulated values, run `ARCHIVE_PREVIEW=1 TCP_SWING_PREVIEW=1 npm run dev` and open `/?district=bass#booths`. Only this explicitly labelled local mode offsets the archived TCP votes. The table shows Labor's side if present, otherwise Liberal's, otherwise the first candidate in the VEC's published pair.

## Deploy on Netlify

Import the repo into Netlify and publish a production deploy using `netlify.toml`. The production deploy is required for the scheduled collector to run automatically; a static upload or GitHub Pages alone cannot run the collector. No token is needed for the VEC's public pages. The scheduled collector and public API use a site-wide Netlify Blobs snapshot.

- Check `/api/results` on the deployed site. Before publication expect `status: "waiting"` and no votes. `collect` starts fetching at 6 pm Melbourne time on election day, then every five minutes over the weekend and every 15 minutes afterwards.
- The collector uses the VEC’s live [2026 lower-house results index](https://www.vec.vic.gov.au/results/2026-results/district-election-page). Run `npm run check:source` to verify the district links, counts and booth pages against the public site. Check `collect` logs on Netlify and compare the deployed dashboard against the corresponding public pages. If the index moves, `VEC_RESULTS_PATH` can override it within the supported official results paths; other path or markup changes require updating `lib/vec.mjs`.
- The service only fetches recognised links on the VEC origin. The collector uses bounded concurrency and shared snapshots. It checks voting-centre pages for active district projections within a separate time budget, prioritising districts least recently checked. The booth view fetches the linked first-preference and TCP pages with CDN caching. Missing or unrecognised pages are surfaced explicitly. Old district rows can be retained and marked stale after a partial read and are excluded from that snapshot's statewide totals. A wholesale outage keeps the last available snapshot labelled stale.
- Share `/?demo=1` for a populated review of six districts, or `/?demo=1&district=bass&view=booths&units=swing` to jump to booth swings. This labelled browser-only demo derives simulated counts from the bundled archive and never calls the live results API. The regular URL uses the live feed. Commit the generated JSON files under `public/data/`; the deployment does not regenerate them.

## 2022 booth baseline

The source is the official VEC [2022 statewide lower-house recheck workbook](https://www.vec.vic.gov.au/-/media/c4665365795d431e811d0ce818e373d4.xlsx). The dashboard roster comes from the [2026 district nominations list](https://www.vec.vic.gov.au/voting/2026-state-election/find-candidates). Rebuild both with `python3 scripts/build-baseline.py`, or check the versioned data with `python3 scripts/build-baseline.py --check`. The historical file contains the workbook SHA-256. Narracan has a 2026 district page but no baseline in this workbook: its election was held separately.

Landing-page swings use the workbook's full formal district totals. Statewide swing is vote-weighted across the same set of currently reporting districts with 2022 totals; statewide Share still includes all fresh results. The demo compares its simulated counts with the same selection of historical booths and pooled vote categories.

Matches require a unique normalised booth label within the same district and vote category. Early and ordinary voting stay separate. No fuzzy match or boundary reconstruction is applied. Votes from unmatched booths, mobile and pooled/declaration categories are excluded from both matched denominators; informal votes never enter a party share. Independent votes stay in first-preference denominators, but the dashboard does not equate different independent candidates across elections. The display reports matched venues and their share of formal votes in the displayed venue rows.

The separate booth TCP baseline is rebuilt with `node scripts/build-tcp-baseline.mjs` from the VEC’s indicative voting-centre counts. It contains 1,729 election-day venues and pooled early TCP across the 87 archive districts. Booth comparisons and booth-based forecasts can use these counts even when the indicative district leader differs from the declared winner, provided the archived pair matches the final pair where one exists. The final district distribution does not provide replacement booth totals. Swing requires the same two sides, matched by party (including across candidate changes) or by candidate name for independents. A changed pair or withheld TCP gives a dash. A TCP-page failure does not suppress first preferences.

### Venue matching sources

- **2022:** The VEC's archived results provide district and booth labels. The Tally Room's [2022 polling places workbook](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1BQRuU-tP8erXJOVxP6jQ9QWZ4qyE4TB7/view) adds venue names, street addresses and latitude/longitude. The VEC's public 2022 polling-location XML provides IDs and names, but no addresses; the VEC's 2022 GeoJSON files describe electoral boundaries.
- **2026:** The VEC's [public venues workbook](https://www.vec.vic.gov.au/-/media/19326aa361284a49a69b08194421da96.xlsx) provides district assignments, voting-location labels, premises and addresses. The [public venue locator API](https://maps.vec.vic.gov.au/api/votingcentres/70531e1c-b7e4-4f29-8875-54df0146c356/) supplies `venueId`, premises, address, latitude/longitude and host district without browser cookies. In a spot check, each request returned only 100 election-day centres; queries centred on different locations returned different sets. Treat it as a coordinate source and check coverage against the workbook. Join premises and addresses back to the workbook to recover result labels and joint-centre district assignments. The [electorate endpoint](https://maps.vec.vic.gov.au/api/electorates/1) lists all 88 district IDs and names.

The booth table uses a unique same-name comparison first unless a site move was flagged, then a nearby or weighted estimate, then the historical district's pooled votes of the same voting type. Open a booth's details to see its first-preference baseline; TCP estimates carry a tilde and tooltip. Early booths use pooled historical early TCP as a district proxy, and a different TCP pairing still has no swing. Narracan compares against its official January 2023 supplementary election. District proxies and spatial estimates are per-booth comparisons and should not be summed.

The district first-preference table's **Swing** compares only reporting booths that have a unique historical booth match of the same voting type. Flagged moves, estimates and proxies are excluded. Each year's party shares are calculated from the sum of votes for the same matched set; Votes and Share still use the full current district count.

Run `python3 scripts/audit-booth-matching.py --map-snapshot booth-map-snapshot.json` for an election-day audit based on the Longman approach. It reports distances and weights in `booth-match-audit.csv`, with weighted first-preference counts and comparable historical TCP counts in `booth-match-estimates.json`. A clear match within 500 m can receive 100%; a lone neighbour out to 5 km is marked as an estimate. For similarly distant sites the script blends at most three neighbours using normalised inverse-distance weights. These are local booth baselines; the same historical booth can inform more than one estimate, so the resulting counts must not be added across booths. The generated files are local review outputs, not dashboard inputs.

Add `--coordinates-only` to select 2022 baselines exclusively by location within the same district, without a same-name preference or fallback. Results go to the `-coordinates` CSV and JSON files. Each year's venue metadata still needs to be joined to that year's booth labels to attach coordinates to vote records.

After running both audits, run `node scripts/build-booth-comparisons.mjs` to regenerate the compiled comparison data, including flags for moved labels and the Narracan supplementary results.
